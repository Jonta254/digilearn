# Editorial review policy

Every current course and downloadable workbook is a **structured draft v0.9**. The
application must not describe a course as reviewed until the following evidence is
recorded for that specific course version:

1. A named subject-matter reviewer checks each learning outcome, lesson, activity,
   knowledge check, project and cited source.
2. A copy editor checks language, reading order, labels and cross-device presentation.
3. A reviewer completes the course as a learner and records defects and resolutions.
4. The release record stores reviewer identity, review date, next-review date, version
   and a short change log.
5. Health, security, legal, regulatory and fast-changing tool content receives a
   shorter review interval based on its risk and rate of change.

The project must never infer approval from a build passing. Automated checks can find
structural defects; they cannot substitute for subject expertise or evidence that an
assessment measures its intended learning outcomes.

## Assessment language

Current quizzes are formative knowledge checks. They support practice and feedback;
they do not certify competence, identity, supervised performance or paid entitlement.
Any future credential needs a documented validity argument, assessment blueprint,
scoring policy, accessibility review and appropriate human oversight.
